Aberdeen winters aren't the worst in Scotland, but they're long, they're wet, and from late autumn the gritters are out on every main road from the A90 to the back streets of Torry. That's good news for getting to work. It's less good news for your car.

This note is about what road salt actually does, where it ends up, and what's worth doing about it, without turning car care into a second job.

## What's actually on the road

The stuff spread on the roads is mostly rock salt, which is salt mixed with grit. The salt lowers the freezing point of water so ice doesn't form or melts faster. The grit gives tyres something to bite on.

Once it's been driven over and rained on, it turns into a grey, salty slush. Every car that goes through it throws some up, and every car behind gets a coating. By January a lot of cars in the city are the same colour from the door handles down, regardless of what colour they started.

## What salt and grit do to a car

**Salt and metal don't get on.** Salt water speeds up corrosion. Modern cars are much better protected than they used to be, but anywhere bare metal is exposed, a stone chip on the bonnet, a scrape on a sill, the edges of panels, salt gives rust a head start.

**Grit chips and dulls.** The grit gets flung at the front of your car and the lower panels at speed. It's why bonnets and bumpers pick up stone chips over the winter, and why the paint low down on a car can look dull by spring.

**Brine sits in the places you don't look.** The salty spray works its way into wheel arches, the underside of the sills, behind the bumpers, the bottom edges of the doors and around the boot lid. Those spots stay wet longer than anywhere else, and that's where the salt sits.

**It gets inside too.** Every time you get in with wet boots, salty water goes onto the carpet. When it dries it leaves white tide marks, and a damp carpet that never quite dries out through the winter can start to smell.

## Where to look

If you want to see how much winter your car is carrying, look at:

- the inside of the wheel arches
- the sills, especially behind the front wheels
- the bottom of the doors and the door shuts
- the lower half of the back of the car, which catches spray from your own tyres
- the driver's footwell, for tide marks on the carpet
- the wheels themselves, where brake dust and salt combine into a stubborn grey crust

## How often to wash it off

You don't need a gleaming car in February. You need the salt not to sit on it for weeks. A good rule of thumb for a car in daily use through the gritting season:

- **every two to three weeks**, a wash that gets the lower half, the arches and the wheels properly
- **soon after** any long run on heavily gritted roads, or after a spell of snow and slush
- **in milder, dry spells** when it's easier to get the car fully dry afterwards

## A note on washing in the cold

There are a few things to be sensible about in winter.

**Freezing temperatures.** Water on a car in freezing weather turns to ice in door seals, locks and on the glass. A wash on a day that's above freezing, or done earlier in the day so the car can dry, avoids that. If it's properly icy, it can wait a day.

**Door shuts and seals.** These hold water after a wash. Drying them stops doors freezing shut overnight.

**Wax helps.** A layer of wax or a wax spray gives salt and grime less to grip onto, so the next wash is easier. It's part of both our Exterior Valet and the Full Works.

## Inside the car

The winter mess inside is mostly water, salt and grit off boots. A few things help:

- **Rubber mats** in winter, if your car has carpet ones. They're easy to rinse and they protect the carpet underneath.
- **Knock your boots** against the sill before you get in. It sounds daft. It works.
- **Don't let it stay wet.** If the carpets are damp, run the heater on the feet with the windows cracked on a dry day.
- **Hoover regularly.** Dry grit in the carpet gets ground in every time you press the pedals.

## The spring clean

Around March or April, once the gritters have stopped, it's worth giving the car one proper clean to get the whole winter out of it. That means the arches, the sills, the wheels, the door shuts, the carpets and the inside of the windows, which pick up a film all winter from the heater.
