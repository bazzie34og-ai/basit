Selling privately, part-exchanging at a dealer, handing a lease car back: in all three, the last few days before the car goes are the ones that make the most difference to how it's seen. This note is a plain checklist. No scare stories, no promises about what anyone will pay or charge you. Just what's worth doing, and in what order.

## Start with your paperwork, not your bucket

Before you clean anything, find out what the other side will look at.

**If it's a lease car,** your agreement or the lessor's return guide sets out how they assess the car's condition when it comes back. Each company has its own standards. Read yours early. It'll tell you what they treat as normal wear and what they don't, and it'll often tell you how the inspection is done and when.

**If you're selling privately,** the buyer is the inspector. They'll look at the car, the service history and the MOT history, which they can check online.

**If you're part-exchanging,** the dealer will appraise the car, usually quickly and with a practised eye.

In all three cases, the person looking at the car is trying to work out its real condition. A clean car makes that easy. A dirty one makes people assume the worst.

## Gather the bits that go with the car

These are easy to forget and awkward to chase afterwards:

- both keys, and the spare if there's one in a drawer somewhere
- the service book or digital service record
- the V5C logbook (for a sale), or the documents your lessor asks for
- the locking wheel nut key, usually in the glovebox or under the boot floor
- the parcel shelf, the boot floor cover, the load cover on an estate
- the charging cables, on an electric or plug-in hybrid
- the owner's manual

A car that comes with everything in its place feels like a car that's been looked after.

## Empty it completely

Before any cleaning starts, take everything out. Glovebox, door pockets, the cubby under the armrest, the boot, under the boot floor, under the seats. You'll find coins, receipts, a single glove, a parking ticket from three years ago. You might also find that missing locking wheel nut key.

This matters for a lease return too. Personal items left in a returned car can be hard to get back.

## The inside: what people actually notice

A buyer or inspector opens the driver's door first. That's where the impression's made.

- **Smell.** Dogs, smoke, food, damp. A smell is the first thing anyone notices and the hardest to ignore.
- **Pet hair.** People without dogs see it instantly. It needs more than a hoover to shift. Our note on [getting dog hair out of a car](/guides/getting-dog-hair-out-of-a-car/) has the detail.
- **The driver's seat and footwell.** The bolster wears, the carpet under the heel gets ground-in dirt.
- **Steering wheel, gear knob and door handles.** Shiny with use. Cleaning these makes a car feel less tired straight away.
- **Inside of the windscreen.** A film builds up from the heater and from people breathing. You only notice it with the sun in front of you, which is exactly when a buyer will be looking.
- **Child seats.** Take them out and look underneath. Bring a bin bag.
- **The boot.** Often the dirtiest part, and the first thing a family buyer opens after the driver's door.

## The outside: honest, not hidden

The outside is about showing the car as it is.

- **Wash it properly,** wheels included. Brake dust on the alloys makes a car look older.
- **Look at the headlights.** Cloudy, yellow lenses age a car in photos. [Headlight restoration](/headlight-restoration-aberdeen/) is £25 with us.
- **Don't try to hide damage.** A clean car makes a dent or a scuff more visible, not less. That's fine. An honest description of a known mark builds more trust with a private buyer than a mark they find themselves. On a lease return, damage is assessed against the lessor's standards whatever state the paint's in.
- **Check the tyres** for tread and obvious damage, as a buyer or inspector will.
- **Top up the screenwash** and check the bulbs. Small things, but a warning light on the dash at a viewing is never a good look.

## Photos for an advert

If you're selling privately, the photos are your first viewing.

- Take them the day the car's been cleaned, in daylight, but not in harsh midday sun.
- Find a plain background. A clean car on a quiet street looks better than one squeezed between bins.
- Shoot the front three-quarter, the back three-quarter, both sides, the dash, the front seats, the back seats and the boot.
- Photograph any marks honestly, close up.
- Blur or cover the number plate if you'd rather not have it online.

## The order to do it in

1. **A week or two before:** read the lease return guide, or decide where you're advertising. Book anything mechanical that needs doing.
2. **A few days before:** gather the keys, documents and loose parts. Empty the car.
3. **A day or two before:** the valet. Close enough that it's still clean on the day, without having sat through a week of Aberdeen weather.
4. **On the day:** a final look round, photos if you're selling, and hand it over.
