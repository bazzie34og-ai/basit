South Aberdeen sits right on the coast. From Torry round past Girdle Ness, through Nigg and Cove Bay and on down to Portlethen and Newtonhill, a lot of homes are close enough to the sea to taste it on a windy day. So are the cars.

Add the commute, the A90 south and the AWPR junctions, the Altens and Tullos industrial estates, and the fact that a lot of Portlethen and Newtonhill heads into the city every morning, and you've got cars that work hard and live in salty air. We come to them, at home or at work.

## What the coast does to a car

**Salt spray.** You don't have to drive through it. On a windy day the spray off the sea carries inland and settles on everything. Cars in Cove Bay and along the coast at Newtonhill pick up a dull salty bloom on the glass and paint, and it comes back quickly after every wash. It's mild, but it's constant, and salt on paint and in the seals isn't something you want sitting there for months.

**Winter on top of that.** From late autumn, the A90 and the AWPR are gritted, and the brine from the road combines with the sea air. The lower half of a commuter car from Portlethen can be grey by Wednesday. The arches and sills hold it longest. Our note on [road salt and grit through an Aberdeen winter](/guides/winter-road-salt-car-care-aberdeen/) covers what it does and how often to wash it off.

**Industrial estates.** Altens and Tullos are busy with heavy vehicles and site traffic. Park there all day and the car collects dust and road dirt, and the wheels pick up a crust of brake dust and grime.

**Gulls.** Torry and the harbour end of the city have more than their fair share. Bird mess is acidic and etches into paint if it's left in the sun. Get it off sooner rather than later.

**Weathered headlights.** Cars that live outside on the coast, facing the weather, often have headlights that have gone cloudy or yellow earlier than they should. [Headlight restoration](/headlight-restoration-aberdeen/) is £25 and can go on top of any service.
