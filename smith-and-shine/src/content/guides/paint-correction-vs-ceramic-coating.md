Paint correction and ceramic coating are often sold together, so it’s easy to think they’re the same thing, or that one does the job of the other. They don’t. One repairs the paint and the other protects it.

Here’s what each actually does, what neither can do, and how to decide what your car needs.

## First, how your paint is built

Most modern car paint has three layers:

- **Primer** on the metal.
- **Base coat**, which is the colour.
- **Clear coat** on top: a thin, clear layer that gives the shine and takes the day-to-day abuse.

Nearly all the marks people want gone, like swirls, light scratches and dullness, are in the clear coat. That matters, because it’s what paint correction works on.

## What is paint correction?

**Paint correction is machine polishing that removes swirls and light scratches.**

Swirls are the fine, circular scratches you see as spider webs around reflections in sunlight. They come from automatic car washes, dirty sponges, wiping a dusty car with a dry cloth, and years of rushed cleaning.

Correction uses a machine polisher, pads and polishing compounds to level the clear coat, taking off a tiny, controlled amount until the swirls and light scratches are gone. Once the surface is flat again, light reflects straight off it, and that’s where the deep, wet-look gloss comes from.

### What paint correction can’t do

This is the honest bit. **Paint correction removes swirls and light scratches. Deep scratches can be improved, but not always removed.**

If a scratch has gone through the clear coat into the colour, or down to the primer, polishing can soften how it looks, but removing it completely would mean taking off too much clear coat. That’s a job for a body shop.

A rough test: run a fingernail across the scratch. If your nail catches, it’s probably too deep to fully polish out.

## What is ceramic coating?

**Ceramic coating is a hard, glossy layer on your paint that makes washing easier and beads water.**

It’s applied as a liquid, panel by panel, and cures into a thin, tough layer bonded to the clear coat. Once it’s on:

- Water beads up and rolls off, taking some dirt with it.
- Dirt, sap and droppings struggle to grip, so they wash off more easily.
- The paint has extra gloss and depth.
- It lasts far longer than a wax or a spray sealant.

### What ceramic coating can’t do

- **It doesn’t remove scratches or swirls.** It can make paint look glossier, but the swirls are still there underneath.
- **It doesn’t stop stone chips.** It’s a thin layer, not armour. For chips, you want paint protection film (PPF).
- **It doesn’t mean you never wash the car.** It means washing is quicker, easier and kinder to the paint.

## Which comes first?

If your car needs both, **correction always comes first.**

A ceramic coating locks in whatever is underneath it. Coat swirly paint and you’ve sealed the swirls in, under a glossy layer that makes them harder to deal with later. Correct first, then coat, and you’ve locked in the best version of your paint.

## Do I need both?

Not necessarily. It depends on your paint and what you want.

**Correction only.** Your paint is swirled or dull, and you just want it looking its best again. Afterwards, regular careful washing keeps it that way. Our [monthly plan](/monthly-valet-plan/) includes hydrophobic paint protection every visit, which gives a lighter version of the water-beading effect.

**Ceramic only.** Your car is new or nearly new, and the paint is already in great shape. Coating it early keeps it that way. New cars, including EVs, are a good time to coat. One Tesla Model 3 owner told us the car looked “better than the day I collected it” after a detail.

**Both.** The paint is swirled, and you want the finish to last. This is the full treatment, and the one that keeps a car looking showroom-fresh for longest.

**Neither, yet.** Sometimes a car just needs a proper [full valet](/full-valet-telford/) or [detail](/car-detailing-telford/). Chris will tell you if that’s the case. He’s not going to sell you a coating your car doesn’t need.

## What about PPF?

[Paint protection film](/paint-protection-film-telford/) is a clear film fitted over the paint, usually on the areas that take the most hits: the bonnet, bumper, wing mirrors and front wings. It takes stone chips so your paint doesn’t. Some people have film on the front and ceramic everywhere else.
