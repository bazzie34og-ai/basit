If you’ve ever looked at a valeting price list and thought “what’s the actual difference?”, you’re not alone. It’s the question we get asked most, usually on a driveway, usually while the kettle’s boiling.

So here it is in plain English: what a mini valet is, what a full valet is, what each one costs, and how to choose between them.

## The short answer

- **Mini valet:** for a car that’s mostly fine and just needs bringing back up to scratch. A wash, a tidy inside, and a polish so it actually shines.
- **Full valet:** for a car that needs doing properly. Every surface, inside and out, with time spent on the bits a quick clean skips.
- **Full valet & deep clean:** for the car that’s been lived in. Kids, dogs, spills, stains and smells.

At Smith & Shine, a [mini valet & polish](/mini-valet-telford/) is from £45, a [full valet](/full-valet-telford/) is from £70, and the full valet & deep clean is from £90. All three are mobile: we come to your driveway, home or work.

## What a mini valet is for

Think of a mini valet as maintenance, not a rescue. It’s ideal when:

- The car was cleaned properly not that long ago.
- The outside is dusty or road-grimy rather than caked.
- The inside needs a hoover and a wipe, not a shampoo.
- You want it looking smart for something: a wedding, a work trip, picking someone up from the station.

A mini valet covers the basics well and finishes with a polish, which is what makes the car look *finished* rather than just *washed*. It’s quicker than a full valet, so it fits round a working day.

What it won’t do is fix things that have built up over months. If there’s a coffee ring on the passenger seat that’s been there since spring, a mini valet isn’t going to shift it. That’s not us being awkward; it’s just a different job.

## What a full valet is for

A full valet is the proper one. It’s the clean most people book first, and the one that makes people ask “have you had a new car?”.

On the outside, it’s a thorough wash with tar removal, wheels cleaned and tyres dressed, plastics restored and a polish. On the inside, it’s everything: a full vacuum including the boot, the dashboard, door cards and console cleaned, the seats cleaned and the windows done inside and out.

Book a full valet when:

- It’s been a few months, or you honestly can’t remember the last proper clean.
- You’ve just bought the car and want a fresh start.
- You’re [selling the car or van](/guides/valet-before-selling-your-car-or-van/).
- The inside is grubby enough that you apologise when people get in.

One customer told us their car was “an absolute state not having been valeted for years” when she bought it. That’s full valet territory. So is the family car with four children’s worth of school runs in it.

## When you need the deep clean instead

There’s a level beyond grubby, and most of us have been there. The deep clean is the full valet plus extra time on the things that really need it:

- Stains on seats and carpets.
- Dog hair woven into the fabric.
- Spilt milk, juice or worse.
- Smells that air freshener just sits on top of.

If any of that sounds familiar, our guide to [pet hair, accidents and smells](/guides/pet-hair-and-smells-in-your-car/) goes into more detail. One customer’s review summed it up: their car was “a tip with ground in dirt, marks and stains (dog owners)” and it went home “gleaming like a brand new car”.

## A simple way to choose

Answer these honestly:

1. **When was it last properly cleaned?** Within the last couple of months: mini. Longer, or never: full.
2. **Would you be happy for someone to sit in the back right now?** Yes: mini. Not without moving three bags and a football: full.
3. **Is there anything on the seats or carpets you can’t just hoover up?** If yes, deep clean.
4. **Is this a one-off or do you want it to stay nice?** If you want it to stay nice, read on.
