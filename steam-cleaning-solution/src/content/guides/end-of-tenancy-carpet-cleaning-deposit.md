Moving out is stressful enough without worrying about your deposit. In Cardiff and Newport, with thousands of people renting, from student houses in Cathays and Treforest to family homes across both cities, we see the same questions at the end of every tenancy.

This guide explains what landlords and inventory clerks actually look at on carpets, the evidence that helps, and a simple checklist you can work through before the keys go back.

It isn’t legal advice. Your tenancy agreement, your inventory and your deposit protection scheme are what count. But it should help you avoid the most common reasons for carpet deductions.

## How deposits work in Wales, in brief

If you rent from a private landlord in Wales, your deposit must be protected in a government-approved tenancy deposit scheme. At the end of the tenancy, your landlord or agent compares the property against the inventory from when you moved in. If they want to keep some of the deposit for cleaning or damage, they need to show why. If you disagree, the scheme offers a free dispute service.

That means **evidence matters on both sides**. The landlord needs to show the carpet was worse than when you moved in. You can show it was cleaned properly before you left.

## What inventory clerks check on carpets

Check-out inspections tend to focus on the same things.

### General cleanliness

Is the carpet clean and hoovered throughout, including under where the beds and sofas stood? Areas that were covered by furniture are often the giveaway, because they look newer and cleaner than the open floor around them, and that contrast shows how much dirt the rest of the carpet holds.

### Stains and marks

Drink spills, food, makeup, nail varnish, candle wax and muddy footprints are the most common. Each is noted with its location, and compared against the check-in report.

### Pet damage and odour

If pets were allowed, clerks look and smell for accidents. Urine soaks into the underlay, and the smell can come back on a damp day long after the surface looks clean. This is where surface cleaning alone often fails.

### Traffic lanes

Grey paths from the door to the sofa and along the hallway. Some of this is fair wear and tear, but much of it is ground-in dirt that a proper clean lifts out.

### Indentations and fading

Dents from furniture feet and fading from sunlight are usually fair wear and tear. They’re worth knowing about, so you aren’t surprised by them on the report.

## The evidence that helps your case

- **Your check-in inventory.** Read it again before you move out. If it says carpets were stained or worn when you moved in, you can’t be charged for those marks.
- **Photos from move-in day**, if you took them.
- **Photos after the clean.** Clear, well-lit photos of each room taken from the doorway, plus close-ups of any marks that wouldn’t shift.
- **An itemised invoice** from a professional cleaner, with the date, the address and what was cleaned.
- **A note of anything permanent.** If a mark was tested and couldn’t be removed, say so in writing. It shows you tried, and helps everyone decide whether it’s damage or wear.

## The move-out carpet checklist

Work through this in the last week of your tenancy.

1. **Read your tenancy agreement and inventory.** Look for any clause about professional cleaning, and note the carpet condition at check-in.
2. **Book the clean for after your furniture has gone.** An empty room gets a better clean, and you won’t drag new dirt in while moving boxes.
3. **Leave a gap before the inspection.** Carpets need a few hours to dry. Don’t book a clean an hour before the inventory clerk arrives.
4. **Hoover everywhere first**, including edges and under radiators, or let us know if you’d like us to do it.
5. **Don’t try strong products on stains.** Bleach, oxygen powders and hard scrubbing can strip colour and turn a stain that would have come out into permanent damage. Blot fresh spills with a white cloth and leave the rest to us.
6. **Point out every mark** when we arrive, including ones from before your time. We’ll test, treat and tell you honestly which will come out.
7. **Photograph each room** once the carpets are dry.
8. **Keep the invoice and photos** until your deposit has been returned in full.

## What about “stains from previous tenants”?

This comes up all the time, especially in student houses. If a mark was there when you moved in and it’s noted on the check-in inventory, it isn’t your responsibility. If it wasn’t noted, it’s harder to argue. Photograph it, point it out to us, and we’ll do our best with it.

Stains of unknown age are the hardest to predict. They may have been treated with the wrong product years ago, or set by heat. We’ll test, treat and be honest about what’s permanent.
