It’s the first thing almost everyone asks us: how long until we can walk on it?

The honest answer is that most carpets cleaned with hot water extraction are dry enough to walk on in a few hours, and fully dry by the end of the day. But the exact time depends on your carpet, your home and, in Wales, the weather. Here’s what makes the difference, and what you can do to help.

## Why carpets are damp after cleaning

We clean carpets with hot water extraction. That means we spray hot water and cleaning solution deep into the fibres, then suck it straight back out with the dirt. A good machine pulls most of the water back out, but not all of it. A little moisture stays in the pile, and that is what needs to dry.

The aim is always to leave the carpet damp, not wet. If a carpet feels soaking after a clean, too much water went in, or not enough came back out. That is when you get long drying times, musty smells, and marks that wick back up from below.

## Typical drying times

These are rough guides, not promises. We’ll always tell you what to expect for your carpet on the day.

- **Short-pile and loop carpets** (common in hallways, offices and rentals) dry fastest, often in a couple of hours.
- **Standard cut-pile carpets** in lounges and bedrooms usually take a few hours.
- **Thick, deep-pile and shaggy carpets** hold more water and can take most of the day.
- **Wool carpets** absorb more moisture than synthetic ones, so they often take a little longer.
- **Stairs** usually dry quickly, because each step has only a small amount of carpet and air moves well on a staircase.
- **Rugs** dry best flat, with air moving over and under them. A thick rug can take longer than the carpet around it.

## What speeds drying up

Drying is about two things: moving air across the carpet, and giving the moisture somewhere to go.

### Airflow

This is the big one. Still air above a damp carpet quickly becomes humid, and humid air can’t take more moisture. Moving air carries it away.

That’s why we bring air movers. They are low, powerful fans that blow air flat across the carpet surface. Where they help, we set them up before we leave and tell you when to switch them off.

At home, you can help by:

- opening a window, even just a crack, on the side of the house away from the wind
- leaving internal doors open so air can move between rooms
- using a normal fan pointed across the floor, not at the ceiling
- running the extractor fan in the kitchen or bathroom if those rooms are next to the carpet

### A little heat

Warm air holds more moisture than cold air. Putting the heating on low while a window is open is the most reliable way to dry a carpet in winter. Heat with no airflow just makes the room steamy.

### A dehumidifier

If you have one, now is the time to use it, with the windows closed. It pulls water out of the air, so the carpet keeps drying even on a damp, still day.

## Drying carpets in Welsh weather

Cardiff and Newport get plenty of rain, and the valleys get more. On a mild, damp day with no wind, the air outside is already full of moisture, so opening a window doesn’t help as much as you might think.

A few tips from our experience:

- **On wet days, close the windows and use heat plus a fan or dehumidifier.** You’re better off drying the air you have than letting damp air in.
- **On cold, dry days, crack a window and put the heating on low.** Cold winter air is often drier than it feels, and warming it up indoors helps it soak up moisture.
- **On warm, breezy days, open up.** Windows on opposite sides of the house give you a through draught, and carpets can dry quickly.
- **Book a morning slot in winter if you can.** The carpet has the whole day to dry before the house cools down at night.
- **Older terraced houses can be slow.** Solid walls and small windows mean less airflow. A fan makes a real difference here.

## What slows drying down

- **Over-wetting.** The most common cause of slow drying is too much water going in. We control how much we use and go over the carpet with extra dry passes when needed.
- **Thick underlay** soaks up any water that gets past the carpet backing. That’s another reason to keep moisture in the pile, not below it.
- **Closed-up rooms.** A bedroom with the door shut and the window closed can take twice as long as the same room with the door open.
- **Putting furniture back too soon.** Feet and legs trap moisture under them, and can leave marks.

## Aftercare: the first 24 hours

1. **Keep outdoor shoes off** until it’s dry. Socks or clean slippers are fine.
2. **Keep pets and little ones off** if you can, until it’s dry.
3. **Keep furniture on protectors** (foil squares or plastic tabs under the legs) until the carpet is fully dry, so wood stain or rust can’t mark it.
4. **Brush the pile** gently with your hand or a soft brush if any areas look flat. The stripes left by the wand brush out as the carpet dries and settles.
5. **Hoover once it’s fully dry.** This lifts the pile and picks up anything loosened during the clean.
6. **Message us if a mark reappears.** Sometimes a stain deep in the underlay wicks up as the carpet dries. It usually responds to a quick spot treatment.
