If you’ve got a dog, you’ve probably been here. An accident on the carpet, you clean it up, it looks fine, and a week later, on a damp day, the smell is back. Sometimes it seems worse than before.

It’s one of the most common reasons people message us, and it’s not because they didn’t clean it properly. It’s because of where urine goes and what it leaves behind. This guide explains why the smell comes back, what makes it worse, and what proper treatment involves.

## Where the urine actually goes

A carpet is three layers: the pile you see, the backing that holds it together, and the underlay beneath. Urine goes straight through the pile and backing, and soaks into the underlay. On a large accident, it can reach the floorboards or concrete underneath.

When you blot the surface, you remove what’s sitting in the pile. The rest is below, out of reach.

## Why the smell comes back

As urine dries, the water evaporates but other things are left behind, including salts and the compounds that cause the smell. Those salts attract moisture from the air. On a humid day, and we have plenty in South Wales, they get damp again and release the smell.

That’s why a spot can seem fine for weeks and then smell strongly after a rainy spell, or after a carpet has been cleaned with water alone. Adding water without removing what’s causing the smell can briefly make it stronger.

## Things that make it worse

Most of these are things people try in good faith.

- **Scrubbing hard.** It pushes urine deeper and spreads it sideways, so a small patch becomes a larger one. It can also fray the fibres.
- **Ammonia-based cleaners.** Urine already smells of ammonia to a dog. Cleaning with it can encourage your dog to go in the same place again.
- **Perfumed sprays and powders.** They cover the smell for a while, but don’t deal with what’s causing it. Some powders also clog the pile.
- **Steam irons and very hot water on fresh stains.** Heat can set the stain and make it harder to remove.
- **Bleach.** It strips colour from carpet and won’t reach the underlay anyway.

## What to do straight after an accident

1. **Blot, don’t rub.** Press kitchen roll or a white towel firmly onto the spot and stand on it. Keep going with fresh paper until almost nothing comes up.
2. **Rinse with a little cool water** and blot again. This dilutes what’s left.
3. **Leave it to dry with airflow.**
4. **Keep your dog away** from the spot so it doesn’t become a habit.
5. **Take a photo** if you want us to look at it later. It helps us see how big it was.

That deals with the surface. If the smell comes back, it’s time for a deeper treatment.

## How we treat it properly

The aim is to reach every part of the carpet the urine reached, break down what’s causing the smell, and then rinse it all out.

### Finding the extent

Accidents are usually bigger underneath than they look on top. We look closely at the area, check the colour of the pile and feel the backing, so we treat the whole affected patch, not just the visible mark.

### Treating at the source

We apply a treatment made for urine, in enough quantity to reach the same depth the urine did. It needs time to work, so we leave it for the right amount of time before going any further.

### Rinsing it out

Then we use hot water extraction. That means we spray hot water and cleaning solution deep into the fibres, then suck it straight back out with the dirt. This time, the water carries out what’s been broken down, instead of leaving it behind to dry back into salts.

### Drying properly

A treated area is wetter than the rest of the carpet, so drying matters. We use air movers where they help, and we’ll tell you how to keep the air moving afterwards.

## Being honest about old accidents

Most pet smells improve a lot with the right treatment. But we’ll always be straight with you about the harder cases.

- **Old, repeated accidents** in the same spot, especially from an elderly dog over months, may have soaked the underlay many times. Sometimes the best fix is replacing the small section of underlay underneath. We’ll tell you if we think that’s the case.
- **Colour changes.** Urine can bleach or yellow some carpets. Cleaning removes the smell and the residue, but it can’t put colour back.
- **Subfloors.** If urine has soaked into wooden floorboards beneath, that smell may need treating separately.

We’ll always patch-test first and tell you what to expect before we start, so there are no surprises.

## Stopping it happening again

- Treat accidents quickly and thoroughly, so there’s no smell left to draw your dog back.
- If your dog is older or has started having more accidents, it’s worth a chat with your vet. Changes in toilet habits can have a medical cause.
- Washable rugs or mats in favourite spots make life easier while you work it out.
