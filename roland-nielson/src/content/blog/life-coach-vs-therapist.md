I'm a life coach. So you might expect me to tell you a coach is all you need. I won't, because it isn't always true, and getting this wrong can cost a man a lot.

Here's the honest answer.

## The short version

**A therapist** is a licensed mental health professional. They can assess and treat conditions like depression, anxiety, PTSD and addiction. They're trained to work with trauma and with what's happening underneath your patterns.

**A life coach** like me is not a licensed professional. Coaching doesn't diagnose or treat anything. It helps a man who is stable enough to function set goals, build habits, follow through and stay accountable. It's focused on what you do next, not on healing what happened before.

Both can help. They do different jobs.

## When you need a therapist (or a doctor) first

See a licensed professional, not a coach, if any of these are true:

- You're having thoughts of harming yourself or someone else. **Call or text 988 right now.**
- You can't get out of bed, go to work or take care of basic things for weeks at a time.
- Trauma, flashbacks or panic are running your life.
- You're drinking or using and can't stop, or stopping makes you physically sick. That can be dangerous to do alone.
- There's violence or fear in your home.
- You think you might have a condition that needs medication or a diagnosis.

A coach is the wrong tool for these, and a good coach will tell you so. When it fits, I refer men to licensed professionals.

## When a life coach is a good fit

Coaching tends to work well when:

- You're functioning, but stuck. Same day on repeat.
- You know what you should do and don't do it consistently.
- You want structure: routines, habits, a plan for the bad days.
- You want someone to hold you to what you said.
- You're rebuilding after a hard chapter and want help with the practical side: work, money, relationships, routine.
- You'd rather talk to a regular guy who's been through it than sit in an office.

## When you might want both

Plenty of men do both, and it can work well. A therapist helps you understand and heal what's underneath. A coach helps you turn that into daily action and keeps you accountable between appointments.

If you already see a therapist, tell them you're working with a coach, and tell your coach what you're comfortable sharing. Coaching should support that work, never compete with it.

## Cost: life coach vs therapist

Therapy is often covered at least partly by health insurance, depending on your plan. Coaching is not covered by insurance because it isn't a medical service.

Coaching prices vary a lot nationally, from under $100 a session to several hundred. My plans run $149 to $399 a month. You can see exactly what's included on my [pricing page](/pricing/). If cost is your main concern and you need clinical care, check what your insurance covers first, or call 211 for low-cost local options.

## What about men who "don't do therapy"?

A lot of men tell me they'd never see a therapist. Sometimes that's pride. Sometimes they had a bad experience. Sometimes they just don't want to sit on a couch and talk about their childhood.

I get it. But I'd push back a little: if you need clinical help, the right professional is worth trying, and there are therapists who are direct, practical and good with men. Don't let a stereotype keep you from help that could change your life.

If you don't need clinical help and just need structure, honesty and someone in your corner, that's what coaching is for.
