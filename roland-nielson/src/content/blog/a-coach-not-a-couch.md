Ask most men if they want to "talk about their feelings" and you'll watch them shift in their seat.

It's not that men don't have feelings. We have plenty. It's that a lot of us picture getting help as lying on a couch, staring at the ceiling and talking about our childhood for months. For some men, that picture is enough to keep them from getting any help at all.

Coaching is a different picture. Less couch, more locker room. Less "how does that make you feel," more "what are you going to do about it this week."

That's why more men are choosing it. Here's what makes it different, and how to tell if it's right for you.

## Talking about the past vs training for next week

The simplest way I can explain coaching: it's training, not fixing.

Some kinds of help spend a lot of time on the past. Where your patterns came from. What happened when you were a kid. Why you are the way you are. That work has real value, and for some men it's exactly what they need, especially if old wounds are running their life.

Coaching starts somewhere else. It assumes you're steady enough to get through your days, and it asks: what do you want next week to look like? Then it helps you build that week, check how it went and adjust.

One looks back to understand. The other looks forward to act. Both can matter. They're just different jobs.

## Why that fits a lot of men

Here's what men tell me they like about coaching, in their own words:

**"I want something I can do."** Most men are wired to solve problems. Give them a task and they'll get after it. Coaching turns big, foggy feelings into small, clear steps. Go to bed by a set time. Put the phone in the drawer at dinner. Make one call tomorrow.

**"I want someone to be straight with me."** A good coach doesn't just nod. A good coach tells you when your plan won't work and asks the question you've been dodging.

**"I don't want to be a project."** Coaching doesn't treat you like something's wrong with you. It treats you like a man who wants to get better at something, the way an athlete has a trainer.

**"I want to see progress."** Every week you report back. Did you do what you said? You can see it working, or see where it's stuck, pretty fast.

## What coaching actually looks like

Coaching isn't a mystery. Here's how it runs with me.

We start with what keeps happening. Maybe you start strong and slip. Maybe you snap at your kids. Maybe you feel like you're on autopilot and lost your purpose somewhere around 40.

We pick two or three things to work on. Each one gets a plan for normal days and a smaller plan for bad days.

Then we meet regularly, by video, by phone or in person. Every session starts with the same question: did you do what you said? We look at what got in the way, plan the next week and go again.

It's built around three simple ideas: make tomorrow easier, stop before you react and do what you said.

## Coaching is not a cop-out

Some men worry that picking coaching is a way of avoiding the "real" work. Sometimes it is. Let me be honest about that.

If you're in a crisis, coaching isn't the right first step. If you're thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 right now. If trauma, depression or an active addiction is running your days, start with a doctor, a mental health professional or a recovery program. That isn't weakness. That's using the right tool.

But if you're functioning and stuck, if you know what to do and you're not doing it, coaching isn't a cop-out. It's a way to finally do the work you've been putting off.

## The stigma is changing

Twenty years ago, a lot of men wouldn't admit they had a personal trainer. Now nobody blinks. Getting help to get stronger is just smart.

Coaching is heading the same way. Asking for help building your life is no different than asking for help building your body. The men who get better aren't the ones who never struggle. They're the ones who stop trying to do it all alone.

I started changing my life in 2011. No single big moment. I just kept showing up. Today I'm a father of four boys, and I've been coaching men since November 2025.

I'm not going to sit back and take notes while you talk. I'm going to ask what you're doing this week, and then I'm going to ask how it went.

## Is coaching for you?

Coaching tends to fit men who:

- Feel stuck but are still showing up to work and home.
- Know what they need to do and keep not doing it.
- Want a plan, not just a conversation.
- Want someone to report to every week.
- Would rather talk to a regular guy who's been through it.
