## The short answer

Across the US, one-on-one life coaching commonly ranges from about **$75 to $300+ per session**. Monthly packages often land somewhere between **$200 and $1,000 a month**. Coaches who work with executives or sell big-name programs can charge far more, sometimes thousands for a package.

Omaha prices tend to sit inside that national range. You'll find some coaches lower and some much higher, depending on who they work with and what's included.

My coaching costs **$149 to $399 a month**. Details below.

## Per session vs monthly plans

There are two common ways coaches charge:

**Per session.** You pay for each meeting. It's flexible, but it makes it easy to skip weeks, and skipping weeks is exactly what kills progress.

**Monthly plans or packages.** You pay a set amount for a set number of sessions per month, often with a minimum commitment. This builds a rhythm. Change comes from consistency, so I think monthly plans fit most men better.

## What drives the price of a life coach

- **Session length and frequency.** Weekly 60-minute sessions cost more than twice-monthly 45-minute ones.
- **Contact between sessions.** Texts, check-ins and extra support add value and cost.
- **Who the coach works with.** Executive coaching is usually priced for company budgets.
- **In person vs online.** In-person sessions can carry room costs. Video and phone usually don't.
- **Program length.** Longer, structured programs often cost more overall but include more support.
- **Credentials and branding.** Some coaches price based on certifications, books or audience size. That doesn't always mean better coaching for you.

## Is a life coach worth the money?

Honest answer: only if you use it.

Coaching isn't magic. You'll still do the work. What you're paying for is structure, an outside view and someone who asks every week whether you did what you said you'd do. For a lot of men, that weekly check-in is the missing piece.

A useful way to decide: look at what the old pattern already costs you. Lost work. Money spent numbing out. Arguments at home. Weekends spent recovering instead of living. If coaching helps you change even one of those, it often pays for itself.

If you won't show up or be honest, it won't be worth it. I'll tell you that on the first call.

## Does insurance cover life coaching?

No. Life coaching isn't a medical service, so health insurance doesn't cover it, and it usually isn't eligible for HSA or FSA funds. Some employers pay for coaching through professional development budgets, so it's worth asking.

<p data-scope>If you need care for a mental health condition or addiction, that's a different service and may be covered by insurance. Read <a href="/blog/life-coach-vs-therapist/">life coach vs therapist</a> for help telling them apart.</p>

## Questions to ask any coach before you pay

1. What exactly is included each month?
2. Is there a minimum commitment? What are the cancellation terms?
3. Who do you usually work with?
4. What happens if I miss a session?
5. What don't you do? (A good coach will be clear about this.)
6. Can I talk to you for free first?
