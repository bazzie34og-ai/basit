Everybody has a plan for Monday. Monday you're rested, the week is clean, and you're going to eat right, hit the gym, call your mom and stop scrolling at midnight.

Then Tuesday shows up.

You slept four hours. The truck needs a part you can't afford. Your boss found the one mistake you made. Your kid has a meltdown in the car and you yell louder than you meant to. By 8 PM you're on the couch with a drink and your phone, and the plan from Monday feels like it was written by somebody else.

That's the day I care about. The first question I ask every man I coach is: **what do you do on a bad Tuesday?**

## Why most plans fail on bad days

Most plans are written by your best self, for your best self. They assume you'll have energy, time and a good mood. Those are the three things you never have on a bad day.

So the plan breaks. And because it broke, you feel like you failed. And because you feel like you failed, you quit the whole thing. Three weeks later you start over with a new plan that also only works on good days.

That loop isn't a character problem. It's a design problem. A plan matters most when you're angry, tired, broke, lonely, or ready to quit. If it doesn't work then, it doesn't work.

## Step 1: Set a floor, not just a goal

A goal is what you'll do on a good day. A floor is the least you'll do on the worst day.

- Goal: work out for an hour. Floor: 10 push-ups before bed.
- Goal: no drinking this week. Floor: no drinking before 8 PM, and text someone if you want to.
- Goal: read to the kids every night. Floor: two minutes of real attention at bedtime, phone in another room.
- Goal: get the budget done. Floor: open the bank app and look.

The floor should feel almost too easy. That's the point. On a bad Tuesday you don't need a big win. You need to not break the chain. Keeping a tiny promise to yourself on a terrible day does more for your confidence than a great workout on a good one.

## Step 2: Know your warning signs

Bad days rarely come out of nowhere. They build. Most men have the same three or four early signs every time:

- Skipping lunch, then getting home starving and short-tempered.
- Staying up too late two nights in a row.
- Going quiet with your partner instead of saying you're stressed.
- Checking out with your phone the minute you get home.

Write yours down. When you notice two of them stacking up, that's your cue to switch to the bad-day plan before the day goes sideways, not after.

## Step 3: Have a 10-minute reset

When the day is already bad, you need something you can do in 10 minutes that changes the direction of the evening. Mine is simple: step outside, walk around the block, drink some water, and decide one thing I'm going to do before bed.

Yours might be a shower, a drive with the radio off, a few minutes in the garage, or sitting in the truck for five minutes before you walk in the door. The rule: you do it **before** you react. Stop before you react. That pause is where most of the damage gets prevented.

## Step 4: Decide who you'll text

Isolation turns a bad day into a bad week. Pick one or two people you'll text when it's going wrong. Not to fix it. Just to say it out loud.

"Rough day. Not going to drink tonight. Checking in."

That's it. Saying it to someone makes it real, and it's harder to break a promise someone else heard. If you have a sponsor, a friend from church, a brother or a coach, put their name in your plan now, while you're calm.

## Step 5: Make tomorrow easier

On a bad Tuesday, the best thing you can do is set up Wednesday. Make tomorrow easier.

- Put the dishes in the sink, at least.
- Lay out clothes for the morning.
- Set the alarm and put the phone across the room.
- Write down the one thing that matters most tomorrow.

None of these take more than a few minutes. All of them mean Wednesday-you starts ahead instead of behind.

## Step 6: Clean up your mess

If you blew it, and sometimes you will, own it fast. Apologize to your wife or your kid without a speech and without "but." "I was short with you earlier. That wasn't fair. I'm sorry."

Then let it go and try again tomorrow. Keep your word. Call your kids. Clean up your mess. Try again tomorrow. That's not a slogan. It's the whole plan.

## Write your bad-Tuesday plan tonight

Grab a notecard and write:

1. My floor for each habit.
2. My three warning signs.
3. My 10-minute reset.
4. The person I'll text.
5. One thing I'll do to make tomorrow easier.

Put it somewhere you'll see it. On a bad day you won't remember the plan. You'll only follow what's in front of you.
