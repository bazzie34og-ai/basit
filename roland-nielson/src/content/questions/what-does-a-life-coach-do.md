A life coach helps you get clear on what you want, build a plan that holds up on real days, and keeps you to that plan by checking in every week. That's the plain version. A coach doesn't hand you a new personality or fix your past. A coach helps you do what you already know you should be doing.

Most men I talk to aren't confused about what to do. They know they should go to bed earlier, put the phone down at dinner, deal with the money, call their kids. The problem isn't knowing. It's doing it on a Wednesday when they're tired and nobody's watching.

That gap between knowing and doing is where a coach works.

## The short list of what a coach actually does

Strip away the buzzwords and a good life coach does five things:

1. **Asks better questions.** What do you want this to look like in six months? What keeps getting in the way? When exactly do you quit?
2. **Helps you pick fewer goals.** Most men try to change eight things at once and end up changing nothing. Two or three is plenty.
3. **Builds a plan for bad days.** Anyone can follow a plan when they're rested. The plan has to work when you slept four hours and the boss chewed you out.
4. **Checks in.** Every week, same question: did you do what you said? No shame, just the truth.
5. **Adjusts.** If something keeps failing, the plan is wrong. You change the plan, not your worth.

That's it. Nothing mystical. It's structure, honesty and someone in your corner who doesn't let you off the hook.

## What a week of coaching looks like with me

Here's how it runs for the men I work with.

We meet by video, by phone or in person. In person means wherever works: your place, a private meeting space set up by appointment, or a walk-and-talk outside. Sessions are 45 or 60 minutes, depending on the plan, and I keep evenings and Saturdays open so working men can make it.

The first session is about where you are and what keeps happening. We pick a couple of commitments. Each one gets a normal version and a bad-day version.

After that, sessions follow a rhythm. What did you do? What got in the way? What's coming up this week that could knock you off track? What's the one thing you'll do before we talk again?

Between sessions, you live your life. That's where the real work happens. The session is just the place where you report back and reset.

## The three things I build with every man

My coaching rests on three ideas. I come back to them in almost every session.

**Make tomorrow easier.** Set up the next day before you go to bed. Clothes out, phone across the room, one clear task written down. Small things that mean you start ahead instead of behind.

**Stop before you react.** Most damage at home and at work happens in about ten seconds. We build a pause into those moments, so you choose what you say instead of regretting it later.

**Do what you said.** Keep your word, starting small. Every kept promise rebuilds a bit of trust, with the people around you and with yourself.

## What a life coach doesn't do

This part matters as much as the rest.

A coach doesn't dig through your childhood or work on deep wounds. A coach doesn't handle a crisis. If you're in a dark place or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988. If you're dealing with a mental health condition or an addiction that's still active, a doctor, a mental health professional or a recovery program is the right first stop. Coaching can come alongside that later.

A coach also doesn't do the work for you. I can ask the question every week. I can't answer it for you.

## Who coaching tends to help

The men I work with are mostly between 30 and 55. Fathers, working men, men rebuilding after addiction or a setback. They say things like:

- I feel stuck in life.
- I start strong, then slip.
- I'm home, but I'm not really there.
- Sober, but my life is still a mess.

If any of that sounds like you, coaching is worth a look. If a wife, mom or brother is reading this for a man in their life, there's a page [for families](/for-families/) that explains how to bring it up.
