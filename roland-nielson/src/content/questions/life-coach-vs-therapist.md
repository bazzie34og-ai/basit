A therapist treats mental health conditions, and a life coach helps a man who is stable set goals, build habits and follow through. If you're struggling to function or carrying trauma, depression or an active addiction, you need a therapist or a doctor first. If you're functioning but stuck, a coach may be the right fit.

I'm a life coach. So you might expect me to say a coach is all you need. I won't, because it isn't always true, and getting this wrong can cost a man a lot.

## The short version

**A therapist** is a licensed mental health professional. Depending on their training, they can assess and treat conditions like depression, anxiety, PTSD and addiction. They're trained to work with trauma and with what's going on underneath your patterns.

**A life coach** like me is not a licensed professional. Coaching doesn't diagnose or treat anything. It's for a man who is steady enough to get through his days and wants help with what he does next: goals, habits, follow-through and someone to report to.

One looks at why. The other works on what now. Both can help. They're different tools.

## When to see a therapist instead

Please see a licensed professional, not a coach, if any of these are true for you:

- You're having thoughts of harming yourself or someone else. **Call or text 988 right now.** If you're in danger, call 911.
- You can't get out of bed, get to work or handle basic things for weeks at a time.
- Trauma, flashbacks or panic are running your days.
- You're drinking or using and can't stop, or stopping makes you physically sick. Quitting some substances alone can be dangerous, so talk to a doctor.
- There's violence or fear in your home.
- You think you might have a condition that needs medication or a diagnosis.

A coach is the wrong tool for any of these, and a good coach will tell you so. When it fits, I point men to licensed professionals, a doctor, a recovery program or 211 for local services. I'd rather lose a client than keep a man in the wrong room.

## When a life coach is a good fit

Coaching tends to work when:

- You're getting by, but stuck. Same day on repeat.
- You know what you should do and you don't do it.
- You want structure: a routine, a plan for the bad days, a weekly check-in.
- You want somebody to hold you to what you said.
- You're rebuilding after a hard chapter and want help with the practical side, like work, money, routine and home.
- You'd rather talk with a regular guy who's been through it.

The men I work with are mostly fathers and working men, roughly 30 to 55. A lot of them aren't in crisis. They're tired of starting strong and slipping. That's coaching territory.

## When you might want both

Plenty of men do both, and it can work well. A therapist helps you understand and heal what's underneath. A coach helps you turn that into daily action and keeps you honest between appointments.

If you already see a therapist, tell them you're working with a coach. Tell your coach what you're comfortable sharing. Coaching should support that work, never compete with it. If your therapist thinks coaching isn't a good idea right now, listen to them.

## The cost difference

Therapy is often covered at least partly by health insurance, depending on your plan. Coaching isn't covered, because it isn't a medical service.

Coaching prices vary a lot, from under $100 a session to several hundred. My plans run $149 to $399 a month, all listed on my [pricing page](/pricing/). If money is your main worry and you need clinical care, check what your insurance covers first, or call 211 for low-cost options near you.

## "I don't do therapy"

A lot of men tell me they'd never see a therapist. Sometimes it's pride. Sometimes they had one bad experience. Sometimes they just don't want to sit on a couch and talk about their childhood.

I get it. I'm still going to push back a little. If you need clinical help, the right professional is worth trying. There are therapists who are direct, practical and good with men. If the first one isn't a fit, try a second. Don't let a picture in your head keep you from help that could change your life.

If you don't need that kind of help, and what you need is structure, honesty and someone in your corner, that's what coaching is for.
