## What life coaching costs across the country

National prices for one-on-one life coaching are all over the place. A common range is about $75 to $300 per session. Monthly packages tend to land between $200 and $1,000. Coaches who work with executives or sell big-name programs can charge thousands for a single package, often paid by a company.

Omaha prices tend to sit inside that national range. Some coaches here charge less and some charge a lot more. It depends on who they work with, how long sessions run and what comes with them.

If a number made your stomach drop, you were probably looking at coaching built for a corporate budget, not a family one.

## Per session or monthly: two ways coaches charge

**Paying per session.** You pay each time you meet. It feels flexible. The catch is that it makes skipping easy. One busy week turns into three, and skipped weeks are exactly where progress dies.

**Paying monthly.** You pay a set amount for a set number of sessions each month. That builds a rhythm. Change comes from showing up again and again, not from one great conversation. For most men I work with, a monthly plan fits better because it keeps them coming back on the hard weeks too.

## What makes one coach cost more than another

A few things push the price up or down:

- **How long and how often you meet.** Four 60-minute sessions cost more than two 45-minute ones.
- **Support between sessions.** Texts and check-ins take the coach's time, so they usually add to the price.
- **Who the coach serves.** Executive coaching is priced for company budgets. Coaching for regular working men shouldn't be.
- **Where you meet.** In-person sessions can carry room costs. Video and phone usually don't.
- **How long the program runs.** A structured three- to six-month program costs more overall but includes more support.
- **Branding.** Some coaches price off their book, their audience or their follower count. A big name doesn't always mean better coaching for you.

## Is it worth the money?

Only if you use it. That's the honest answer.

Coaching isn't magic. You still do the work. What you're paying for is structure, an outside view and somebody who asks every week if you did what you said.

Here's a simple way to think about it. Look at what the old pattern already costs you. Money spent numbing out on the weekends. Missed work. Fights at home that eat a whole evening. If coaching helps you change even one of those, it may cover itself. I go deeper on this in [is life coaching worth it](/is-life-coaching-worth-it/).

If you won't show up or won't be honest, it won't be worth it. I'll tell you that on the first call.

## Will insurance pay for it?

No. Life coaching isn't a medical service, so health insurance won't cover it. It usually isn't eligible for HSA or FSA money either.

Some employers pay for coaching out of a training budget. It's worth asking HR.

If what you're dealing with is a mental health condition or an addiction that's still active, that's a different kind of help, and insurance may cover it. Your doctor can point you in the right direction.

## Questions to ask any coach before you pay

Whoever you hire, ask these first:

1. What exactly do I get each month?
2. Is there a minimum commitment, and how do I cancel?
3. Who do you usually work with?
4. What happens if I miss a session?
5. What don't you do? A good coach can answer this fast.
6. Can I talk to you for free before I pay anything?

If a coach dodges these, keep looking.
