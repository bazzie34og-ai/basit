Life coaching is worth it if you're functioning but stuck, you'll show up every week, and you'll tell the truth about what you did. It's not worth it if you want someone else to do the work, you won't be honest, or what you really need is deeper help first. That's the honest answer, from a guy who sells coaching.

I'd rather you hear that from me now than find it out three months and a few hundred dollars later.

## When coaching tends to be worth it

These are the situations where I've seen coaching pay off.

**You know what to do, and you don't do it.** You've read the books. You've downloaded the apps. You've started over on a Monday more times than you can count. Information isn't your problem. Follow-through is. That's exactly the gap coaching is built for.

**You need someone to report to.** A lot of men keep promises to everyone but themselves. Once somebody is going to ask, "Did you do it?", things change. Not because you're scared of the coach. Because saying it out loud makes it real.

**You're rebuilding.** After a divorce, a job loss, a stretch of drinking or just a few lost years, it helps to have someone help you sort out what to fix first. Starting over at 45 is a lot easier with a plan.

**Your reactions are costing you.** If you snap at your family and regret it, coaching can help you build a pause and a plan for those moments. Stop before you react sounds simple. It takes practice and someone checking in.

**You're ready now.** Not someday. Not after the holidays. Coaching works best when a man is tired of the old pattern and wants something different badly enough to show up on a rough week.

## When coaching isn't worth it

Here's where I'd tell you to save your money.

**You want someone to fix you.** I can ask the question every week. I can't do the push-ups, have the hard talk with your wife or put the bottle down for you. If you're hoping a coach will carry it, it won't work.

**You won't be honest.** If you're going to tell me everything went great when it didn't, we'll spend the time pretending. That's expensive pretending.

**You can't show up.** Coaching runs on rhythm. If you'll cancel every other session, the plan never gets tested.

**You need a different kind of help first.** If you're in crisis, dealing with trauma that's running your days, or caught in an addiction that's still active, start with a doctor, a mental health professional or a recovery program. If you're thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 now. Coaching can come later, once you're on steadier ground.

## What makes it work, if you do it

The men who get the most out of coaching tend to do a few things:

1. **They pick two or three goals, not ten.** Narrow beats ambitious.
2. **They plan for bad days.** Every commitment has a smaller version for when life goes sideways.
3. **They tell the truth in session.** Missed it? Say so. That's the useful part.
4. **They stay long enough.** A few weeks shows you the pattern. A few months starts to change it.
5. **They keep the plan simple.** Make tomorrow easier. Do what you said.

None of that requires being special or disciplined from the start. It requires showing up.
