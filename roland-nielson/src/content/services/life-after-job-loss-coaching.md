Losing a job hits a man in places he didn't know he had. The money is the obvious part. The quieter part is waking up with no reason to get dressed and feeling like you let your family down.

I've started over more than once, from further back than most. Nobody hands you a plan for that. You build one, a day at a time.

## Why the weeks after a layoff go sideways

In the first week, most men are busy. They update the résumé, send a pile of applications and tell themselves it'll be quick. By week three the replies are slow, the days are long and the couch starts winning.

That slide isn't weakness. You lost the structure that held your days together, and nothing replaced it. Coaching after job loss puts the structure back first, so the search has something to stand on.

## What changes with a plan and a weekly check-in

You stop measuring the day by how bad you feel and start measuring it by what you did. Five applications. Two calls. One walk. Dinner with the kids, phone away.

At home, the tension eases because your wife can see a plan, not just a worried man. And when the right job does come, you walk in steady instead of desperate.
