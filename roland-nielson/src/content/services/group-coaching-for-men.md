Ask a man how many friends he has and he'll give you a number. Ask him who he'd call at midnight if his marriage was falling apart, and the number gets smaller. Sometimes it's zero.

That's not because men don't want connection. It's because nobody set a table.

## Why going it alone stalls

When you're the only one who knows your goals, it's easy to let them slide. Nobody notices. A coach helps with that. But there's something different about sitting with a few other men who are fighting the same fights and hearing them tell the truth.

You realize you're not the only one who snaps at his kids, drinks a little too much on Fridays or feels lost at 40. That alone takes a lot of weight off.

## How a group is different from a men's night out

A men's night out is fun, and it's mostly talk. A coaching group has a purpose. Every man is there to change something, and every meeting follows the same shape: report back, work through what got in the way, set the next step.

I lead it. My job is to keep it honest, keep it on track and make sure the quiet guy gets heard and the loud guy doesn't run the room.
