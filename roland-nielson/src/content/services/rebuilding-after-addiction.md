I grew up in North Omaha with a mom who struggled with meth and a dad who wasn't around. I spent time in juvenile detention and carried that chaos into adulthood. I lived addiction.

In 2011 I started changing how I lived. There was no single big moment. I kept showing up, kept my word more often and learned to sit with a feeling without letting it run the house.

I'm not hiding my story to make anybody comfortable.

## What a sober coach in Omaha actually does

Getting clean is a beginning. What you build around it is what keeps it. Meetings and a sponsor are there for your recovery. I'm there for the rest of your week.

If you have meetings, a sponsor, a recovery program or an aftercare plan, keep every one of them. Coaching works next to them. We focus on routines, relationships, work and the goals you've been putting off.

Want local meetings and groups too? My list of [Omaha resources for men](/omaha-resources/) is a good place to start.

## Why "sober but still a mess" is so common

Nobody hands you a plan for the day after. You stop using, and suddenly there's a lot of time, a lot of guilt and old friends a phone call away. That's not failure. It's the next stage, and it needs its own plan.

## Your story is real. So is your comeback.

Recovery is yours, and I can't promise outcomes. Men who stay with it often notice the same things: days have a shape, there's a written plan for the worst hours, and family starts believing them again, slowly, because they're consistent.
