Every blowup has a moment right before it. A tightness in the chest. Heat in the face. A voice in your head that says, "Here we go again." Most men only notice the wreckage after. That moment is where we work, because catching it gives you a choice.

## Why you keep snapping at the people you love

It's almost never the thing in front of you. It's the job you can't yell at, the bills you can't fix tonight, the sleep you didn't get. By the time you walk in the door, the tank is full. Then somebody leaves a backpack on the floor and it all comes out on them.

You save your worst for the people who feel safest. Not because you love them less, but because they'll still be there. That's why it hurts afterward.

I grew up around a lot of yelling and chaos. Learning to stop before I react is still something I practice as a father of four boys.

## A pause and a plan

Stop-before-you-react coaching is simple on paper. Notice the warning signs early. Use a short pause you've practiced. Have a plan for what you'll do next. Repair when you miss.

Simple isn't easy, so we meet weekly, look at what happened and adjust. You don't need a new personality. You need a few seconds and a better move.

## What your family notices first

The house gets quieter, but not in the bad way. Your kids stop flinching when your voice rises. Your wife brings things up instead of holding them in. And when you do mess up, you clean it up fast, which teaches your kids more than any lecture ever could.
