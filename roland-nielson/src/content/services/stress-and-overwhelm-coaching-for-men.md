Most men I talk to aren't lazy and they aren't weak. They're overloaded. They took on one more thing, then another, and now the week is a pile of promises they can't keep. So they run faster, sleep less and feel behind anyway.

I learned early in life what it's like when everything is urgent. Growing up, there wasn't much calm in my house. As a father of four boys, I've had to build calm on purpose, because it doesn't show up on its own.

## Why working harder doesn't fix it

When you feel behind, the natural move is to grind. Stay late. Answer email at dinner. It works for a week, then the list fills back in, because the list was never the real problem.

The real problem is a week with no edges. Nothing ends, so nothing feels finished. Stress and overwhelm coaching for men is about putting the edges back: a time the workday stops, things you no longer say yes to, and space that belongs to you and your family.

## What a lighter week looks like

It doesn't mean doing less of what matters. It means doing less of what doesn't. You close the laptop and actually leave. Your wife knows the plan for the week before Monday hits. You have one evening a week with nothing scheduled.

The change isn't dramatic. It's quieter. Fewer late-night emails. Fewer blowups at the dinner table. More mornings where you start ahead instead of behind.
