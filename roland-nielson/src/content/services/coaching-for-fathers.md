I didn't have a dad around. I know what that absence does to a kid, and I know how easy it is to repeat a version of it without meaning to. You can be in the house every night and still be gone.

Maybe you come home wrung out and snap at small things. Maybe you and your wife haven't had a real conversation in months. None of that makes you a bad man. It makes you a tired one without a plan.

## Why good dads drift

Work takes the best hours of your day. By the time you pull into the driveway, you've got nothing left, and the people who matter most get the leftovers. Nobody decides to be that dad. It happens a little at a time.

Then there's what you carry from your own childhood: the tone, the silence, the way your father handled stress. Some of it you swore you'd never repeat.

## What changes when you have a plan

I'm a father of four boys. Being present with them matters more to me than looking like I have it all figured out. That's what I help other dads work toward.

It takes a few specific habits, practiced on purpose, with someone asking every week how they went. The walk-in gets calmer. Dinner has a real conversation in it. Your kids start coming to you with stuff, because you've been listening.

You start liking the dad you're becoming. Your partner feels like she has a teammate again.
