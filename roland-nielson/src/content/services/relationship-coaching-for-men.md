Nobody decides to become roommates. It happens a little at a time. A new job, a new baby, a mortgage, a few hard years. You both get busy surviving, and one day you notice you only talk about who's picking up whom.

Most men respond by working harder, going quiet, or hiding in the garage. It feels like keeping the peace. To her it feels like being left alone in the marriage.

## Why it keeps drifting

Men are often taught to fix things, not to sit with them. So when she brings up a problem, you jump in with a solution, or you get defensive. She stops bringing things up. You take the quiet as a sign things are fine. They aren't.

Add stress from work, short sleep and a phone in every free minute, and there's nothing left over for each other. The love may still be there. The attention isn't.

## Working on your side of it

Relationship coaching for men starts with you, because you're the only one you can change. We work on listening, on bringing up hard things without blowing up, and on keeping the small promises that rebuild trust.

You'll practice real conversations, try them at home, and report back. Some will go well. Some won't. We'll learn from both.

## What the house feels like after

You laugh together again, sometimes. Hard talks get shorter and less heated. You stop dreading the evening. It's not a movie ending. It's two people who choose each other again on ordinary days, and that's worth the work.
