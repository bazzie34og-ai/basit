A lot of men I talk to are good at their jobs. They run a crew, close deals, fix what's broken and keep people on track. Then they get home and switch off. Somebody else runs the house, and they just try not to get in trouble.

It's not that they don't care. It's that work gives them a playbook and home doesn't.

## Why work feels easier

At work, the rules are clear. There's a goal, a schedule, a boss and a paycheck. You know when you're winning. At home, the goals are fuzzy, the feedback is emotional and nobody gives you a performance review until there's a fight.

So men pour their best energy where they feel competent. Home gets what's left over. Over the years that gap grows, and one day you realize your kids know your work voice better than your real one.

## Leadership starts with the man in the mirror

The best leaders I've known did the boring stuff first. They kept their word. They didn't blow up. They admitted mistakes before anybody caught them. That's personal leadership, and it works the same in a break room and a kitchen.

When you lead yourself well, people trust you more everywhere. Your team feels it. Your wife feels it. Your kids definitely feel it.

## What we actually do

We pick a few behaviors that matter in both places and practice them every week. A shutdown routine before you drive home. A pause before you answer when you're heated. One hard conversation you've been dodging.
