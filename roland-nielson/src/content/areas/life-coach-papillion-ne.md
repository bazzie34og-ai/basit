There's no office to drive to. We meet by appointment in the spot that fits your week.

A lot of Papillion dads like the walk-and-talk. We meet at Walnut Creek, do a slow lap or two and go over what happened since last time: the promise you kept, the night you lost your cool and the plan for the next few days. Moving helps. Men say more when they aren't staring at each other.

When it's cold or the week is packed, we sit down at your place if you have a quiet room, or at a private meeting space I set up. And after bedtime, video does the job without another trip out of the house.

## For the dad who lives in the bleachers

I'm a father of four boys. I know the season where every weekend is a game and every weeknight is a drive somewhere.

Here's what I've learned. Being at everything isn't the same as being present for it. You can sit through every inning with your head at work and your thumb on your phone. The kids feel it, even if they never say so.

So we work on small things that change that. A short reset in the car before you walk in. One night a week where you're fully home. A plan for the moment your temper runs short at the end of a long Saturday. Nothing fancy. Just kept promises that add up.
