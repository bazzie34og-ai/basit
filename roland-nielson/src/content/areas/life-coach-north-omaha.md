I don't run a storefront office. We meet where it makes sense for you, by appointment.

For a lot of men up here, the walk-and-talk is the best part. Some guys don't open up sitting across a table, and that's fine. Side by side, moving, the hard stuff comes out easier. We pick a park, keep a steady pace and talk about the week: what went right, what slipped and what you're going to do before we talk again.

If you'd rather sit down, we can meet at your place if you have a private room, or at a private meeting space I set up for the session. And if your week is packed with work, kids and family, video and phone keep you consistent without adding another drive. Plenty of men mix all three.

## Why North Omaha matters to me

I grew up here. My mom struggled with meth. My dad wasn't around. I spent time in juvenile detention and carried that chaos into my twenties. In 2011 I started changing how I lived, one kept promise at a time.

So when a man from North Omaha tells me what his week looks like, I don't need the long version. I know the streets, the family pressure and the voice that says nothing ever changes around here. It does. It changed for me, and I've watched it change for other men who decided to keep showing up.
