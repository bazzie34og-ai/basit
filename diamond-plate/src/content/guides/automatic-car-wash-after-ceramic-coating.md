It's one of the first questions people ask after a coating: "Can I still use the car wash down the road?" The answer is yes, technically, but it's not the best idea, and the reason isn't what most people think.

## The short answer

**The chemicals in a car wash won't harm your ceramic coating. The brushes are the problem.** Brush car washes cause swirl marks in the paint, and that's true whether the car is coated or not. A hand wash is best.

## Why the brushes matter

Automatic brush washes use big rotating brushes or cloth strips that slap against the car at speed. They're used on every car that comes through, and they pick up grit along the way. When that grit is dragged across your paint, it leaves fine circular scratches called swirl marks. You'll see them most on dark paint in direct sun, like a spider web around a light reflection.

A ceramic coating helps the paint resist light marks, and because dirt doesn't bond as hard, there's less grit to drag around. But no coating can prevent swirls or wash marks. Repeated brush washes will still mark the paint underneath.

And once swirls are in the paint, a coating doesn't take them away.

## What about the chemicals?

Many people worry that car wash detergents will strip a coating. With Diamond Plate coatings, the chemicals used in car washes won't harm the coating. Both Diamond Plate coatings were independently tested by CSIRO for chemical resistance to ASTM D1308-20, including hydrochloric acid and ethanol, with the result of resistance to discolouration, blistering and cracking.

So chemicals aren't the reason to avoid the car wash. Brushes are.

## Your options, from best to worst

### 1. Hand wash (best)

Rinse first, wash with a pH-neutral shampoo and a clean mitt, rinse again and dry gently. On a coated car this is quick, because water beads straight off and dirt lets go easily. Fortnightly or weekly is ideal. Our [aftercare guide](/guides/ceramic-coating-aftercare/) walks you through it.

### 2. Touchless car wash (OK in a pinch)

A touchless or "no-brush" automatic wash uses high-pressure water and detergents without physical contact. Since the chemicals won't harm the coating and there are no brushes, it's a reasonable option when you can't hand wash. It won't get the car as clean as a careful hand wash.

### 3. Hand-wash bay at a self-serve car wash

Fine, with care. Use the rinse wand freely, but think twice about the foam brush hanging on the wall. It's been used on hundreds of muddy utes. Bring your own mitt.

### 4. Brush car wash (avoid)

The chemicals are fine. The brushes cause swirls. Avoid them if you can.

## A few more tips

- **Use town water** where you can. Bore and rain water can leave mineral spots.
- **Use a bug and tar remover** for splatter and tar instead of scrubbing harder.
- **Don't dry-wipe dust** off the paint with a cloth. That's a fast way to make wash marks.
- **Get droppings off quickly**, especially in summer.

## Why it matters more in Queensland

Brisbane cars get washed a lot. Storms leave grime, the sun bakes on droppings and sap, and anyone near the bay is rinsing off salt. More washes means more chances for brushes to mark the paint, which is why the choice of how you wash matters more here than in a lot of places.

The good news is that a coated car needs less effort to get clean. Most of the dirt comes off with a rinse and a gentle hand wash, so the pull of the drive-through car wash is weaker than it used to be.

## Using a hand car wash business

If you'd rather pay someone to wash it, a hand car wash can work well. Before you hand over the keys, it's worth asking a couple of questions:

- **Do they use clean mitts and fresh water** for each car, or one bucket for the day?
- **What shampoo do they use?** pH-neutral is what you want on a coated car.
- **Do they dry with clean, soft towels**, or a chamois that's been on the ground?
- **Do they use brushes anywhere?** Wheels are fine. Paint isn't.

Tell them the car is ceramic coated. A good operator will adjust.

## Wheels and trim

If your package included alloy wheel protection, the same rule applies: brake dust and grime come off more easily, and a gentle clean is all it needs. Keep stiff brushes for the tyres, not the paint.

## What if you've already used a brush wash?

Don't stress about one trip. The coating isn't harmed by the chemicals. Just switch to hand washing from here on. If you can see fine swirls in the paint, know that a coating won't remove them.

## The bottom line

You can take a ceramic coated car through a car wash and the coating will be fine. Your paint may not be, if the wash uses brushes. Hand wash when you can, use a touchless wash when you can't, and avoid brush washes altogether. That's how you keep the gloss you paid for.
