"Graphene" has become one of the most used words in car care marketing. You'll see it on sprays, waxes, shampoos and professional coatings, often next to some very large claims. So what is it, and is a graphene ceramic coating actually different from a regular ceramic coating?

This guide keeps to what we can honestly say, without superlatives we can't back up.

## First, what is graphene?

Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice, a bit like a honeycomb, one atom thick. That hexagon is why the Diamond Plate logo is a hexagon made of graphene cells.

In the lab, graphene is studied for its unusual properties. In a car coating, it's used as an ingredient in the formula. It's important to understand that a graphene coating isn't a sheet of pure graphene laid over your car. It's a coating that contains graphene.

## What is a ceramic coating?

A ceramic coating is a liquid that's applied to clean, prepared paint and cures into a hard, very thin, slick layer bonded to the clear coat. The "9H" you'll see on many coatings refers to a pencil hardness rating, and it describes the cured coating layer, not your paint underneath.

The main things you notice with any good ceramic coating are:

- A hydrophobic surface, so water beads and runs off.
- Dirt, droppings, sap and grime release more easily.
- Easier, quicker washing.
- Deep, lasting gloss.

## So what does graphene add?

A graphene ceramic coating is a ceramic coating with graphene infused into it. It's still a ceramic coating. Different makers use graphene in different ways and make different claims about what it does.

Our position is simple: we won't quote you unsourced percentages or lab numbers. What we can tell you is what Diamond Plate makes and what's been independently tested.

- **Graphene Ceramic 9H+** is Diamond Plate's graphene-infused ceramic coating and our main product. It's what we apply in every Brisbane package.
- **Ceramic 9H** is Diamond Plate's standard ceramic coating.
- Both are **made in Australia** by Diamond Plate's own R&D team.
- **CSIRO independently tested both coatings for chemical resistance** to ASTM D1308-20. The tests included hydrochloric acid and ethanol, and the result was resistance to discolouration, blistering and cracking.

Note what that test covers: chemical resistance. CSIRO didn't test scratch resistance or UV performance, and it doesn't certify or approve products. If you see a coating sold as "CSIRO approved", ask what was actually tested.

## Graphene coatings from a bottle vs professional coatings

There's a big difference between a graphene spray from the supermarket and a professionally applied graphene ceramic coating. Sprays are a quick top-up that sits on the surface. A professional coating is applied to properly prepared paint and cures into a bonded layer, and it comes with a warranty. Both can be "graphene". They're not the same thing.
