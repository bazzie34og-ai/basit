Ceramic coating and paint protection film (PPF) both get called "paint protection", and both get sold as the answer to keeping a car looking new. But they're very different things that solve different problems. Picking the right one depends on what you're actually trying to protect against.

We'll be straight with you: PPF is better at some things, and ceramic coating is better at others. Here's the honest comparison.

## What each one is

**Ceramic coating** is a liquid that's applied to the paint and cures into a hard, very thin, slick layer bonded to the clear coat. It's hydrophobic, so water beads and runs off and dirt releases more easily. Diamond Plate's main coating is Graphene Ceramic 9H+, a ceramic coating infused with graphene, made in Australia.

**PPF** is a clear, flexible film, usually polyurethane, cut to shape and stuck onto the paint. Because it's a physical layer with real thickness, it can absorb impacts that would otherwise chip the paint.

## Stone chips: PPF wins

This is the clearest difference, so let's get it out of the way. **PPF is better against stone chips.** A ceramic coating is far too thin to absorb a stone flicked up by a truck on the M1 or the Bruce Highway. Film is designed for exactly that.

If your main worry is chips on the bonnet, bumper and mirrors, maybe because you do a lot of highway kilometres or tow, PPF on those areas is the tool for the job. No honest coating business should tell you otherwise.

## Gloss: ceramic coating wins

A ceramic coating adds depth and a glassy, wet-look gloss to the paint underneath. Two layers deepen it further. Film is clear, and good film looks very good, but adding gloss isn't its main job, and edges and seams can be visible up close.

## Easy washing: ceramic coating wins

This is where most owners feel the difference every week. A ceramic coated surface is slick and hydrophobic. Water beads straight off, dirt lets go with less scrubbing, and droppings, sap and bug splatter are easier to remove before they etch. Washing takes less time and less effort.

Less scrubbing also means fewer chances to put wash marks into the paint. To be clear, no coating prevents swirls or wash marks, but it helps resist light marks and makes gentle washing easier.

## Price: ceramic coating wins

Full-body PPF is a much bigger spend than a ceramic coating for most cars, because of the material and the hours of skilled fitting involved. Even partial film on the front of a car is a significant cost.

Neither one removes scratches or swirl marks that are already in the paint.

## Which do you need?

### Choose ceramic coating if:

- You want your car easier to wash and quicker to clean.
- You want deeper, lasting gloss.
- Your main enemies are sun, droppings, sap, storms and salt air.
- You want strong protection at a fixed, sensible price.
- You drive mostly around town.

### Choose PPF if:

- Stone chips are your number one concern.
- You do a lot of highway or gravel-road driving.
- You have a high-value car where chip repair on the front end would be expensive.

### Consider both if:

You want chip protection on the front and the easy-wash gloss of a coating everywhere. Some owners film the bonnet, bumper and mirrors and coat the rest. Our packages are ceramic coatings; we don't install film, so you'd use a separate PPF installer for that part.

## For 4WDs and utes

Big vehicles that go off-road face scratches from scrub and chips from gravel, which a coating won't stop. What a coating does do on a 4WD is make mud, red dirt and bug splatter wash off far more easily. See our page on [4WD and ute ceramic coating](/4wd-ute-ceramic-coating/).

## The bottom line

PPF is armour. Ceramic coating is a slick, glossy, easy-clean shield against the everyday stuff. For most Brisbane cars, especially new and near-new cars driven around town, a ceramic coating gives you the most of what you'll notice day to day, for the least money. If chips are your real worry, get film on the front, and be honest with yourself about it.
