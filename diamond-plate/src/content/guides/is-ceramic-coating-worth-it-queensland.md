Ask ten car owners whether ceramic coating is worth it and you'll get ten answers. Some swear by it. Some think it's an expensive wax. The truth sits in between, and in Queensland the answer leans more towards "yes" than in most places, for reasons that have nothing to do with marketing.

This guide looks at what Queensland actually does to car paint, what a coating can and can't do about it, and how to decide for your own car.

## What Queensland does to car paint

### The sun

South-east Queensland gets a lot of strong sun, all year. Cars parked outside, on driveways, in work car parks, at the shops, spend hours in it every day. Heat and UV are hard on clear coat over time, and they make every other contaminant worse, because heat bakes things on.

### Summer storms

Brisbane's afternoon storms dump water, dust and debris on cars, then the sun comes out and dries it all on. If the paint is bare, those water spots and grime films cling. If it's coated, water beads and runs off, taking much of the dirt with it.

### Bat and bird droppings

Flying foxes and birds are part of life in Brisbane, especially under fig trees, near parks and along the river. Droppings are acidic and, on hot paint, can mark the clear coat surprisingly fast. The single most useful thing you can do is remove them quickly. A coating makes that much easier, because they don't bond as hard to a slick surface.

### Salt air

Live near the bay at Wynnum, Manly, Cleveland or Redland Bay, or tow a boat to the ramp, and your car collects a fine salt film. It holds moisture, helps grime stick and slowly dulls the finish. A coated surface rinses clean far more easily.

### Sap, dust and road grime

Add tree sap from street trees, red dust after a trip west and bug splatter from the highways, and Queensland paint has a lot to deal with.

## What a ceramic coating actually does

A ceramic coating bonds to your clear coat and leaves a hard, slick, hydrophobic surface. In practice, that means:

- **Water beads straight off.** One of our customers put it exactly that way.
- **Dirt and contaminants release more easily.** Washing takes less time and less scrubbing.
- **The gloss lasts.** Two layers deepen it further.
- **It adds a protective layer** between the paint and the things above, so you have more time to wash them off before they do damage.

Diamond Plate's coatings have also been independently tested by CSIRO for chemical resistance, to ASTM D1308-20. The tests included hydrochloric acid and ethanol, and the result was resistance to discolouration, blistering and cracking.

## What it doesn't do

It's just as important to know the limits. A ceramic coating:

- **Doesn't remove scratches or swirl marks.** Whatever is in the paint stays there.
- **Isn't scratch-proof.** It helps resist light marks, but no coating prevents swirls or wash marks.
- **Doesn't stop stone chips.** For that, look at paint protection film. Our [ceramic coating vs PPF](/guides/ceramic-coating-vs-ppf/) guide explains the difference.
- **Doesn't mean you never wash the car.** It means washing is quicker and easier.

If someone tells you otherwise, ask them to put it in writing.

We won't give you a number, because nobody can honestly promise one. What we can say is that paint condition is one of the first things a buyer or a dealer notices. A car that has been kept clean, glossy and free of etched droppings tends to make a better impression than one that hasn't. A coating makes that easier to achieve. ## When ceramic coating is worth it

A coating makes the most sense when:

- **The car is new or near new.** You're protecting good paint, not trying to fix tired paint. Our [new car paint protection](/new-car-paint-protection/) page explains why the first weeks matter.
- **The car lives outside.** Driveway, street or work car park.
- **You'll keep it a while.** The longer you own it, the more washes you save.
- **You hate washing.** Or you love a clean car but don't have weekends to spare.
- **You live near the coast, under trees, or drive a lot of highway kilometres.**

## When it might not be

- The paint is already heavily scratched or faded and you were hoping a coating would fix it.
- Stone chip protection is your main concern. PPF is the better tool.
- You're selling the car next month.
