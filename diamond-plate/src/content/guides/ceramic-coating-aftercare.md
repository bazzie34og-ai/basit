A ceramic coating makes a car much easier to look after, but it isn't a licence to ignore it. A little care, especially in the first week, keeps the gloss deep and the water beading for years. Here's everything you need to know, in the order you'll need it.

## The first {{touchDryMinutes}} minutes

When the applicator finishes, the coating is touch-dry in about {{touchDryMinutes}} minutes. That means it won't smear if you brush against it. It doesn't mean it's fully cured. Before you drive off anywhere, the applicator will walk you through the car and explain what was done. That's the time to ask questions.

## The first {{noWashDays}} days: don't wash it

This is the most important rule. **Don't wash the car for {{noWashDays}} days.** The coating keeps curing after it's touch-dry, and washing too early, especially with shampoo or a brush, can affect the finish.

During those {{noWashDays}} days:

- **Normal driving is fine.**
- **No car washes**, no shampoo, no pressure washer.
- **Park under cover if you can**, away from trees that drop sap or attract birds.
- **Bird droppings:** don't leave them, but don't scrub either. Gently lift them with plenty of water and a soft microfibre cloth. If you're unsure, call us on {{phone}}.

## The first wash

After {{noWashDays}} days, you can wash the car normally. This is where you'll start to notice the difference: water beads straight off and the dirt lets go with far less effort.

A good routine:

1. **Rinse first.** Let the water take off loose grit before anything touches the paint.
2. **Use a pH-neutral car shampoo.** Harsh detergents aren't needed on a coated car.
3. **Wash by hand** with a clean wash mitt, working from the top down.
4. **Rinse again** and let the water sheet off.
5. **Dry gently** with a clean, soft drying towel.

## How often to wash

**Fortnightly or weekly** is ideal. A coated car doesn't need more scrubbing, it needs regular gentle washes so contaminants don't sit on the paint for long. Wash more often if:

- You live near the bay or have been to the beach or boat ramp (salt).
- The car parks under trees (sap and droppings).
- You've done a long highway run (bugs).
- There's been a storm followed by sun (dried-on grime).

## The water you use

**Town water is preferred over bore or rain water.** Bore and rain water can carry minerals that leave spots when they dry. If you're on tank or bore water at home, give the car a final rinse with town water where you can, or dry it promptly.

## Bugs, tar and droppings

- **Bug and tar remover:** use one for bug splatter on the bumper and mirrors, and tar flicked up behind the wheels. The coating makes these come off much more easily, but a dedicated remover helps with the stubborn bits.
- **Bird and bat droppings:** remove them as soon as you can, ideally the same day. They're acidic, and on hot paint in the Queensland sun they can bake on quickly. The coating gives you more time and makes them lift more easily, but it isn't a reason to leave them.
- **Tree sap:** soften it with water and lift it gently rather than scraping.

## Car washes: hand wash is best

The chemicals used in a car wash won't harm the coating. **The brushes are the problem:** brush car washes cause swirl marks, coated or not. A hand wash is best. If you're short on time, a touchless wash avoids the brushes. More in our guide on [taking a ceramic coated car through a car wash](/guides/automatic-car-wash-after-ceramic-coating/).

## A quick check every few weeks

You don't need special products to keep an eye on the coating. After a wash, look for three things:

- **Is water still beading and sheeting off?** If it's beading less, the surface may have a film of road grime on it. A thorough hand wash usually brings it back.
- **Any spots or marks?** Look along the bonnet, roof and boot lid in good light, where droppings and sap tend to land.
- **Lower panels and behind the wheels.** Tar and grime build up here, especially after highway driving. A bug and tar remover sorts it out.

If anything looks wrong, take a photo and call us. It's easier to talk it through than to guess.

## What to avoid

- Brush car washes.
- Dry-wiping a dusty car with a cloth (that's how wash marks happen).
- Harsh, high-alkaline cleaners and degreasers on the paint.
- Leaving droppings, sap or salt to sit.
- Washing in the first {{noWashDays}} days.

## What a coating won't do, even with perfect care

A ceramic coating won't stop stone chips, and no coating prevents every swirl or wash mark. It helps resist light marks and makes careful washing easy. Look after it and it'll keep looking good.
