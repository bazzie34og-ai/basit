You've done the test drive, agreed on a price and you're about to sign. Then the business manager slides across one more option: paint protection. It might be called a coating, a sealant, a protection pack, or something with "nano" or "ceramic" in the name. It might be bundled with fabric protection and a window etch.

Sometimes it's a good product. Sometimes it isn't. The trouble is that at the moment you're asked, you usually don't have enough information to tell the difference. This guide gives you the questions to ask, so you can compare dealer paint protection with a ceramic coating on facts, not pressure.

## What "dealer paint protection" can mean

There's no single standard. Depending on the dealership, paint protection might be:

- A sealant or polymer applied at the dealership's prep bay.
- A ceramic-type coating applied by a third-party contractor.
- A product sold mostly on its warranty, with the coating itself a secondary detail.

None of these is automatically good or bad. What matters is the product, the preparation, who applies it and what the warranty actually promises.

## The five questions to ask the dealer

Write these down, or screenshot them. Ask for the answers in writing.

### 1. Which product is it, exactly?

Get the brand and the product name. "Our premium paint protection" isn't an answer. Once you have a name, you can look it up and compare.

### 2. Who applies it, and how is the paint prepared?

Is it applied by a trained applicator, or by whoever is in the prep bay? Is the paint washed, decontaminated and clayed first? A coating bonds to whatever is on the surface, so preparation is a big part of the result.

### 3. What are the warranty conditions?

This is where a lot of the value is hidden, and where a lot of the fine print lives. Ask:

- How long is the warranty?
- What exactly does it cover, and what does it exclude?
- **Is there a cap** on what the warranty will pay out?
- Is it with the dealer, the product maker or an insurer?

### 4. Are annual inspections required?

Some paint protection warranties are only valid if you bring the car back for inspections, often every year. Miss one and the warranty can lapse. Ask whether inspections are needed, how often, where, and whether they cost anything.

### 5. Is reapplication required?

Some products need a top-up coat at intervals to keep the warranty alive. Ask whether reapplication is required, how often, and what it costs.

## "The dealer left micro-scratches"

It's a common complaint: a new car arrives with fine marks from a quick dealer wash. Be clear about what a coating can do here. A ceramic coating protects the paint as it is. It doesn't remove scratches or swirl marks. If you can see marks at delivery, point them out to the dealer before you drive away, and tell us about them when you book.
